From sketch to sign

Every Lit Neon sign starts life as a drawing with dimensions on it, and nothing is made until you've approved that drawing. Here is what happens before and after.

The making, in order

Stage 1

Measured drawing

Your words are set in the lettering you chose and measured: letter height, overall width and height, and the path the tube will follow down the centre of each stroke.

You get the drawing by email with every dimension marked, plus the estimated length of LED flex. Ask for changes until it's right.

Why we insist on itA sign is cut and bent to the drawing, so this is the moment to catch a spelling, a size that won't fit the wall, or a colour that fights the room.
Stage 2

LED neon flex

LED neon is a flexible strip of LEDs sealed inside a silicone tube that diffuses the light into one continuous line. It is shaped to follow the drawing, letter by letter, then fixed to the backing.

It runs on low-voltage power from a plug-in adapter, so there is no glass, no gas and no high-voltage transformer.

What that means in useThe tube stays cool enough to touch, it won't shatter if knocked, and it dims smoothly from the remote.
Stage 3

Acrylic backing

The tube is mounted on acrylic, either cut to follow the outline of the lettering or as a clean rectangle on polished standoffs. Clear acrylic disappears against the wall; black, white, gold mirror and silver mirror make the panel part of the design.

Cut to shape or rectangle?Cut to shape is lighter and sits flush. A rectangle gives you a panel edge and standoffs, which suits logos and reception walls.
Stage 4

Wired and tested

The sign is wired to a single clear cable that exits bottom right, then lit and checked against the drawing before it's packed with its adapter, dimmer, remote and fixings.

In the boxSign, 12 V adapter with UK plug, dimmer and remote, 2 m clear cable, wall fixings and a copy of the drawing.
Stage 5

Delivered

Packed flat and tracked to any UK address, free. You see the arrival window before you pay; it skips weekends and bank holidays.

LED neon and glass neon

Both make a line of light. They are different things, and it's worth knowing which you're buying.

LED neon flex (what we make)Traditional glass neon
What it isLEDs in a flexible silicone tube on an acrylic backingHand-bent glass tubes filled with gas, lit by a high-voltage transformer

Power and safety, plainly

Low voltage

The sign itself runs at 12 V. The adapter plugs into a normal UK socket and does the conversion, like a laptop charger.

Cool and quiet

LED flex gives off little heat and no noise. It is fine on painted walls, wood, plaster and fabric backdrops.

Indoors and out

Standard signs are for indoor use. Choose the weatherproof option for porches, gardens and marquees; the adapter always stays indoors or in a dry enclosure.
